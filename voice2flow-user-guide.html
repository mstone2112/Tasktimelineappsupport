Voice2Flow — User Guide

Voice2Flow is a flow-diagram app you can talk to. Say "Add a decision with Yes and No connectors" and the shape appears, wired up and ready. Say "Rename the selected shape to Review request" and it's renamed. You direct every step, so your diagram ends up exactly the way you want it. Build a process one command at a time, start from a template, or mix voice, touch, mouse and keyboard however suits the moment.

What Makes Voice2Flow Special

You direct, Voice2Flow does the work — Voice2Flow is a voice-driven editor, not an automatic diagram generator. You tell it what to add or change (a shape, a connector, a label, an owner, a lane) and it does that instantly. That keeps you in control and your results predictable. For a head start, begin with one of the seven templates or the empty swimlane structure, then shape it with your voice.

Try Saying… — You never have to guess what the app understands. The Try Saying… panel on the right lists every phrase the app knows, grouped by what you're doing: Adding Shapes, Editing Shapes, Owners, Connectors, Layout, Swimlanes, Pages & Drill-down, Notes & Links, Group Selection, Item Numbers, and Undo & Redo. Tap any phrase to run it, or read it aloud and say it your own way. In Cards view, each pattern shows the blanks you fill in (the shape, the item number, the lane name); in List view, you see every example phrase one by one.

Edit with your voice, not just create — Voice2Flow isn't only a dictation tool for starting a diagram. You can come back to any diagram and keep editing it by speaking: rename shapes, change their type, set owners, relabel connectors, reverse an arrow, swap two steps, move a shape into another swimlane, align a group, and undo anything you didn't mean.

Say it once for many shapes — "Add a 5-step process in swim lane 1." "Make items 1 through 5 divided processes, make items 6 and 10 decision shapes." "Connect shapes 1 through 4." One sentence can build or change a whole row of shapes.

Every shape has a number — Shapes are numbered, so you can point at them by voice: "Set the owner of item 3 to Sales," "Move item 3 below item 5," "Drill into item 3."

Notes and links on any shape — Attach a note or a web, email or phone link to a shape by speaking or by touch, and optionally include the notes as a numbered list in your PDF.

Swimlanes, phases, and multi-page diagrams — Organize a process by team (swimlanes) and by stage (phases), and drill down from any shape into its own sub-process page.

Templates to start from — Approval Request, Employee Onboarding, Order Fulfillment, Customer Support Ticket, Incident Response, Decision Flow, and Company Org Chart.

Works with draw.io and Visio — Import draw.io and Visio files, and export back out to draw.io XML or Visio, as well as PDF, PNG, and print.

Your files stay with you — Diagrams are saved as .v2flow files in the place you choose. No account is needed. Save to iCloud Drive and your diagrams are waiting on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Getting Started

The Start Screen

When Voice2Flow opens, you can:

  • Blank Diagram — start with an empty page.
  • Open… — open a saved .v2flow file.
  • Import a draw.io or Visio file… — bring in a diagram made in another tool.
  • Or start from a template — begin with an empty swimlane structure or one of the seven templates listed above.

Allow the Microphone

The first time you use voice, your device asks for permission to use the microphone and speech recognition. Please allow both if you want to speak your commands. If you decline, you can still do everything by touch and typed commands.

The Screen at a Glance

Top toolbar — New, Open…, Save, Save As…, and Export. The file name is shown in the middle; an orange dot beside it means there are unsaved changes. Look: Classic / Glass changes the app's appearance: tap it to switch, or press and hold for a menu.

Library (left) — A drawer of shapes you can place by hand: core flowchart shapes, standard symbols, and an extra collection of 91 shapes including BPMN, UML, and icons.

Canvas (center) — Your diagram. Tap a shape to select it; drag to move it; drag its handles to resize it.

Try Saying… and Tools (right) — The phrase list described above, with Tools beneath it: Undo, Redo, Copy, Cut, Paste, Delete, and Page guides.

Command bar (bottom) — The microphone button, the transcript of what you said (or the result of the last command), a picker for the target shape, and a keyboard button for typing a command instead of speaking.

Page tabs (very bottom) — One tab per page ("Page 1", and so on), with a + to add another.

Speaking Commands

How It Works

  1. Tap the microphone button in the command bar.
  2. Speak one command at a time, in plain English, such as "Add a decision with Yes and No connectors."
  3. The app shows what it heard and what it did. If it didn't understand, it offers "Did you mean…?" suggestions you can tap.

The Command history disclosure in the command bar lists what you've said so far in the session.

Voice recognition is currently in English (US).

Typing Instead

Tap the keyboard button in the command bar to type a command. Typed commands use exactly the same phrases as spoken ones, which makes them useful in a quiet room — or a loud one.

Tips for Better Results

  • Use the shape names that appear in the Library: "divided process," "predefined process," "decision," "hexagon."
  • Refer to shapes by their item number ("item 3," "shape number 7") or select a shape first and say "the selected shape."
  • Think in steps. Break a big idea into short commands ("Add a process," "Add a decision with Yes and No connectors"), use multi-shape commands like "Add a 5-step process in swim lane 1," or start from a template. If a request is too general, the app offers "Did you mean…?" suggestions.
  • If a command doesn't work, open Try Saying… and look for the pattern closest to what you meant.
  • Made a mistake? Say "Undo that."

Working by Hand

Voice is the fastest way to get started, but you're always free to take over.

Adding and Moving Shapes

Open the Library on the left and tap or drag a shape onto the canvas. Drag a shape to move it. Alignment guides appear as you drag to help you line shapes up. Drag the handles around a selected shape to resize it.

Selecting Several Shapes

On a Mac, drag a rectangle on the canvas. On an iPad or iPhone, press and hold on an empty part of the canvas, then drag to draw the selection box. Everything inside is selected, and the "selected shapes" voice commands apply to it.

Copy, Cut, Paste, Delete, Undo, Redo

Find all of these under Tools on the right.

Connectors

Connect shapes by voice, or by hand. Connectors can be solid, dashed or dotted, with arrowheads at either or both ends, and can carry a label such as "Yes" or "No."

Page Guides

Turn on Page guides under Tools to see where a printed page will begin and end, so you can arrange your diagram to fit.

Notes and Links

Each shape can carry a note and a link.

  • Note — Select a shape. A small note icon appears on it; tap it to type or edit the note. Or just say "Add a note to item 3: check with finance."
  • Link — A web address, an email address, or a phone number. A small link badge appears on shapes that have one; tap it to open the link. Or say "Link item 3 to example.com."
  • Notes and links travel with your diagram — in .v2flow files, draw.io files (as a tooltip and link), and Visio files (as Shape Data and a hyperlink).
  • In Export → PDF, turn on Include notes to add a numbered list of your notes after the diagram. Links in the PDF are clickable.

Swimlanes, Phases, and Pages

Swimlanes

Add swimlanes to show who is responsible for each step — "Add three vertical swim lanes," "Rename Lane 1 to Sales." Shapes can be added directly into a lane by name ("Add two decision shapes in the Sales lane") and moved between lanes ("Move item 3 to lane 2"). Say "Change all of the owners to match the current lane" to make each shape's owner match the lane it sits in.

Phases

Phases divide the diagram into stages — Plan, Build, Review. You can resize them by voice.

Multiple Pages and Sub-processes

A diagram can have many pages; use the page tabs at the bottom. For a detailed step, say "Create a sub-process for item 3." The shape gets a small badge — tap it, or say "Drill into item 3," to open the sub-process page, and say "Go back" to return.

Org Charts

Start from the Company Org Chart template, select a person, and say "Add a subordinate" or "Add two subordinates to item 3."

Saving, Opening, and Importing

  • Save and Save As… write a .v2flow file to a place you choose.
  • Open… opens a saved .v2flow file (with Browse… to find files elsewhere).
  • Import draw.io / Visio… brings in draw.io files and Visio .vsdx and .vstx files, including files with multiple pages. Voice2Flow brings in as much as it can — shapes, text, connectors, and notes.

Using Voice2Flow on All Your Devices (iCloud)

Voice2Flow keeps your diagrams in ordinary files, so iCloud Drive can sync them between your iPhone, iPad, and Mac. One purchase covers all three.

  • Turn it on once: sign in to the same Apple Account on each device and make sure iCloud Drive is on (Settings → your name → iCloud → Drive, or System Settings → Apple Account → iCloud → iCloud Drive).
  • Save to the Voice2Flow folder. On Mac, Save As… opens in your Voice2Flow iCloud folder automatically. On iPhone and iPad, choose Save, then pick iCloud Drive → Voice2Flow in the location list.
  • Open it anywhere. Use Open… and choose the same folder. On iPhone and iPad it is in the Files app under iCloud Drive; on Mac it is in Finder under iCloud Drive.

Tips: wait a moment after saving on one device before opening the file on another. If a file shows a cloud icon, it is still downloading. Voice2Flow does not merge edits, so if you change the same diagram on two devices before they sync, one version can replace the other — close a diagram on one device before editing it on another.

Exporting and Printing

Choose Export in the top toolbar:

  • Print… — AirPrint on iPhone and iPad; the standard print dialog on Mac.
  • PDF… — A PDF of your diagram, with a preview first. Multi-page diagrams get a heading on each page ("Page 2 of 5"). Choose Include notes to add the numbered notes list.
  • Image (PNG)… — A PNG image. Multi-page diagrams export as a zip of images.
  • draw.io XML… — Open in draw.io (diagrams.net); each page becomes a tab.
  • Visio (.vsdx)… — Open in Microsoft Visio; each page becomes a Visio page.

The export preview lets you choose the paper size and how the diagram is split across pages before you export.

Troubleshooting

The app doesn't hear me. Check that microphone and speech recognition are allowed for Voice2Flow in your device's Settings (iPhone and iPad) or System Settings → Privacy & Security (Mac).

The app heard me but didn't understand. Look at the Did you mean…? suggestions, or find the closest pattern in Try Saying….

I described a whole process and nothing like it appeared. Voice2Flow builds from specific commands rather than general descriptions. Try "Add a 5-step process in swim lane 1," start from a template, or look in Try Saying… for the closest pattern.

A command did the wrong thing. Say "Undo that," or tap Undo under Tools.

Voice recognition is in the wrong language. Voice2Flow currently recognizes English (US).

I can't find my file. Use Open… → Browse… to look in the folder where you saved it.

My diagram isn't showing up on my other device. Check that both devices use the same Apple Account with iCloud Drive turned on, that the file was saved in the Voice2Flow iCloud folder, and give it a minute to sync.
